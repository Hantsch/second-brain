Keeping a knowledge base honest

A knowledge base earns trust one citation at a time. Every claim it makes should trace back to a real note, written by a real person, on a real day — not to a guess dressed up as an answer. The moment a system starts inventing plausible-sounding facts to fill a gap, its usefulness collapses, because nobody can tell the difference between the parts that were checked and the parts that were made up.

This is why a fetched source that yields nothing readable has to say so, loudly and specifically, rather than quietly turning into an empty page. A cookie banner, a paywall, or a mostly-empty single-page app shell all return a perfectly valid HTTP 200, but none of them are a source in any meaningful sense. Treating that response as if it were an article would let a hollow page slip into the vault, indistinguishable from a real one until someone happens to open it and finds nothing there.

The same discipline applies to duplicates. Ingesting the same page twice should not silently create two near-identical entries, nor should it silently refuse the second attempt forever. The honest middle path is to say plainly that the source already exists, and to offer a deliberate way to ingest it again if that is truly what the person wants — a choice made in the open, not a decision the software makes quietly on someone's behalf.
